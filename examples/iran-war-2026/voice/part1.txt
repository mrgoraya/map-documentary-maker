On Saturday, February twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six, American and Israeli jets struck Iran. Within hours, Iran's Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei, was dead. A war began that still has not ended. How did we get here?

Rewind to June twenty twenty-five. Israel and Iran fought a twelve-day war. The United States bombed three nuclear sites: Natanz, Fordow and Isfahan. A ceasefire held. The nuclear fight did not end.

In September, U.N. sanctions came back, ten years after the nuclear deal.

In January, protests swept Iran. A rights group says more than two thousand people were killed. Trump told the protesters: help is on its way.

Then the warships came: two American aircraft carriers.

Talks went on, through Oman. On February twenty-seventh, Oman's foreign minister said Iran had agreed not to stockpile enriched uranium. A deal, he said, was within reach.

That same day, according to the Pentagon, Trump gave the final order.

The next morning, Operation Epic Fury began. Iran fired back with missiles and drones, at Israel, and at Arab states that host American troops.

Then Iran reached for its most powerful weapon. Not a missile. A narrow strip of sea.
