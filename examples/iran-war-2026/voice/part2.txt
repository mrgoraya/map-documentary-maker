Iran's Revolutionary Guard declared the Strait of Hormuz closed. About a fifth of the world's oil normally sails through here. A Guard adviser threatened to set ablaze any ship that tried to pass.

On March first, an Iranian missile killed nine people in Beit Shemesh, Israel. The same day, a drone killed six American soldiers in Kuwait. They were the first U.S. combat deaths of the war.

In southern Iran, a strike hit a girls' school in Minab. Iranian officials say at least a hundred and sixty-five died, mostly children. An early Pentagon review reportedly pointed to an American missile.

Hezbollah joined the war from Lebanon. And on March eighth, Iran named a new Supreme Leader: Khamenei's son, Mojtaba.

Oil passed one hundred dollars a barrel, for the first time since twenty twenty-two.

On April seventh, Pakistan brokered a two-week ceasefire. But talks in Islamabad, led by J.D. Vance, failed. So on April thirteenth, the U.S. Navy began blockading Iran's ports.

On June seventeenth, both sides signed a fourteen-point memorandum: stop fighting, reopen Hormuz, lift the blockade.

It lasted three weeks.
