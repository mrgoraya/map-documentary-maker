On July seventh, a Qatari gas tanker was hit near Hormuz. Iranian state TV said a tanker had ignored warnings. The next day, Trump said the deal was over.

On July fourteenth, the blockade came back. Iran fired at U.S. bases. In Jordan, three American soldiers were killed.

After nearly two weeks of nightly American strikes, both sides paused in late July.

In August, Iran said it had agreed with Oman on a temporary shipping lane. But with the blockade still in place, Hormuz did not fully reopen.

In early September, fighting flared again. The U.S. says it hit three Iranian oil tankers, after Iran fired missiles at American warships.

Sailors paid a heavy price. The United Nations says at least twenty-five seafarers have been killed in and around Hormuz since February.

Iran offered a seven-day plan to reopen the strait. On September twenty-sixth, Trump rejected it.

The war kept spreading. In October, a Houthi missile from Yemen hit Riyadh's main airport. Saudi authorities said twelve people were killed.

In Israel, an election on October twenty-seventh left no clear winner. And in November, the strikes came back.
