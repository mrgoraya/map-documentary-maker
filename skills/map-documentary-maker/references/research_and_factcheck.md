# Research and fact-check

## Sources, best first

1. Primary records: official statements, government and UN documents, court filings, published texts of agreements.
2. Wire services and major outlets with corrections policies: Reuters, AP, AFP, BBC, Al Jazeera, NYT, Washington Post, The Guardian, Bloomberg, NPR, CBS.
3. Specialist outlets: USNI News, Military Times, Foreign Policy, the IISS, think-tank trackers.
4. Encyclopedias only for background, never as the only source of a number.

Write down every source's publication date. For fast-moving stories, the newest reliable report wins, but keep older figures if the script says "at the time".

## The two-source rule

Every number, date, name and claim in the narration needs **two independent sources** (two outlets quoting the same press release count as one). If only one side says it, the script says so: "Iran says...", "according to the Pentagon...". Casualty counts are always attributed and called incomplete where they are.

Conflicting figures go in the brief's "Conflicting figures" table with a recommended wording (a range, or both claims attributed).

## Clipping quotes

Clippings on screen are quotations: copy the sentence word for word from the article you opened, with outlet, date and URL. Never paraphrase into quote marks.

## Independent fact-check (always)

After the brief and draft script exist, start a **separate subagent** with only the script, the brief's reference list and this instruction:

> Open each source. For every number, date, name, place and quote in the script, say CONFIRMED (with the source line), WRONG (with the correct value and source) or UNSUPPORTED. Look especially for: numbers that changed in later reports, claims stated as fact that only one side made, quotes that don't match word for word, and dates off by one day. Don't trust the brief; check the pages.

Fix every WRONG and UNSUPPORTED item in the script (attribute it, correct it or cut it), then add a "Fact-check log" to the brief listing what changed. Only then show the script to the user.

## Map facts

- Coordinates: look them up (lon, lat) for every pin; don't place pins by eye.
- Routes for real events follow reported paths; paths you can't source are drawn as illustrative and said so in the description ("some ship and drone paths are illustrative").
- Disputed borders and names: use Natural Earth's defaults and say so if the story depends on them.
